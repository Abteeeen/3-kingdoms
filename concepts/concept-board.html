Three directions for 3 Kingdoms

三国冒菜 is a Sichuan mao cai counter on Level A of Uptown, Brisbane. It has no website: every online order goes through a delivery app at 22–34% above counter prices, and the most common thing reviewers say is that it is hard to find. Each concept below solves the same problems in a different visual world. Pick one, or tell us what to change.

The sketches are first screens at desktop proportions. Each has its one signature interaction working. Dish photos are drawn placeholders until the shoot.

Type

Archivo, condensed for dish names and stretched wide for prices. Noto Sans SC Black for Chinese. Prices sit in fixed positions and never jump.

Motion

The panels flicker on once, like a board powering up. "Find us" is a scroll-scrubbed walk from the mall entrance to the counter, cut from our own clips.

Why it fits

It fixes the two biggest problems, being hard to find and having no clear menu, inside the look itself. It looks like where the food actually comes from.

Risk

It lives or dies on food photography. Weak photos in lit panels look cheap, so it needs a proper food shoot.

Type

Noto Serif SC for Chinese and Newsreader for English. The only brush lettering is their real logo, so there are no fake brush fonts.

Motion

One ink-bleed reveal as the logo lands. Scrolling scrubs a top-down clip of red oil spreading across the broth. Still image for reduced motion.

Why it fits

It has the strongest continuity with what they already have: the logo, the black-and-red packaging, and the Instagram. It is the most premium of the three.

Risk

It is the most familiar. Din Tai Fung opens with calligraphy on black, and dark food sites are common. It needs one great close-up broth shot.

Concept C

Broadside

A letterpress street poster for the bowl, printed on takeaway-bag kraft. Every line fills the width, and red ink lands a hair off-register. As loud as their Instagram, but about the food.

Type

Big Shoulders Display as condensed wood type, ZCOOL QingKe HuangYou for Chinese bands, and Zilla Slab for body text.

Motion

One press-pull as the poster loads. Bands rewrap into condensed type on phones. No 3D, so it is the lightest and fastest of the three.

Why it fits

The most shareable for creators and students, and it can use rougher handheld clips as "window" panels in the bands.

Risk

Xi'an Famous Foods already works in this poster style, and kraft can drift towards a craft-burger look. It shows fewer dishes at once than Light Box.

The safe option

The category standard, played straight: red and gold, a large food photo, "Order now", menu cards, serif headings. It is what most rivals look like. It works, and it blends in.

What happens next

  1. Choose A, B, C or the safe option, or ask for changes to one.
  2. Share our four Drive clips (each is over the 10 MB limit we can fetch), so we can check which concept the footage suits.
